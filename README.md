# Oral-and-Airway-Fungal-Genome-Catalog
A comprehensive cultivation- and metagenome-derived genome catalog of the human oral-airway mycobiome reveals spatial heterogeneity and disease-specific signatures
Background: While the human gut mycobiome has garnered significant attention, the fungal communities inhabiting the oral cavity and airways remain largely underexplored. The lack of a comprehensive, high-quality reference genome catalog for the oral-lung axis severely limits functional and species-level metagenomic resolution, masking the potential roles of these fungi in host health and disease.
Methods: To comprehensively characterize the genetic diversity of the oral and airway mycobiome, we integrated large-scale targeted cultivation from 506 samples with deep mining of over 20,000 publicly available metagenomic datasets and 205 reference isolate genomes. Using this approach, we constructed the Oral and Airway Fungal Genome Catalog (OAFGC). We subsequently performed functional profiling, comparative genomics against the gut mycobiome, and large-scale ecological and disease-association analyses across 19,997 samples. 
Results: The OAFGC comprises 620 high-quality fungal genomes representing 254 species, including 105 previously undescribed species that substantially expand the known diversity of the human oral–airway mycobiome. Functional analysis revealed profound evolutionary divergence from the gut mycobiome; oral fungi exhibit a remarkable enrichment in oxidative stress metabolism, vitamin biosynthesis, and complex glycolipid assembly, reflecting unique adaptations to the highly oxygenated and fluctuating local microenvironment. Biogeographical analysis demonstrated strong spatial niche differentiation: Candida and Aspergillus dominate fluid-rich habitats (e.g., oropharynx and sputum), whereas Apiospora bloom specifically in hypoxic biofilm environments like dental plaque. Furthermore, disease perturbations (e.g., caries, cancer, and immune-mediated diseases) reshape local fungal communities in a highly disease-specific manner. Machine learning models utilizing these fungal signatures achieved robust diagnostic performance (AUC > 75%) across multiple independent cohorts. 
Conclusions: The OAFGC provides an unprecedented genomic resource that fills a critical cognitive gap regarding the human oral-airway mycobiome. Our findings highlight the profound spatial heterogeneity and functional specialization of these communities, offering novel insights into cross-kingdom microecological interactions and promising non-invasive diagnostic targets for various diseases.
